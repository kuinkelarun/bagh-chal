# Bagh Chal - Complete Game Rules

## Overview

Bagh Chal (बाघ चाल) is a traditional abstract strategy board game from Nepal. The name literally means "Tiger Move" in Nepali.

## Players

- **2 players** (asymmetric gameplay)
- **Player 1**: Controls 4 Tigers (🐅)
- **Player 2**: Controls 20 Goats (🐐)

## Board

- **5×5 grid** of 25 intersection points
- Points are connected by:
  - **Horizontal lines** (all rows)
  - **Vertical lines** (all columns)
  - **Diagonal lines** (at specific points):
    - Four corners: (0,0), (0,4), (4,0), (4,4)
    - Center point: (2,2)
    - Edge midpoints: (0,2), (2,0), (2,4), (4,2)

## Setup

- **Tigers** start at the four corner points
- **Goats** start off the board (will be placed during the game)

## Gameplay

The game has two distinct phases:

### Phase 1: Placement Phase

**Duration**: First 20 goat placements

**Goats' Turn**:
- Place one goat on any empty intersection point
- Cannot move goats that are already placed
- Must place all 20 goats before entering movement phase

**Tigers' Turn**:
- Can move to any adjacent empty point (along connecting lines)
- Can capture goats by **jumping** over them
- Captured goats are removed from the board

**Turn Order**:
- Goat places → Tiger moves → Goat places → Tiger moves → ...

### Phase 2: Movement Phase

**Duration**: After all 20 goats are placed

**Both Players**:
- Can move their pieces to adjacent empty points (along lines)
- Pieces can only move one space at a time (except tiger captures)

**Tigers**:
- Continue to move and capture goats
- **Capture** by jumping over an adjacent goat to the empty point beyond
- Multiple captures in sequence are **NOT** allowed (unlike checkers)
- Can move without capturing

**Goats**:
- Can only move to adjacent empty points
- **Cannot** capture tigers
- Cannot jump over pieces
- Goal is to trap all tigers

## Capturing (Tigers Only)

**How to Capture**:
1. Tiger must be adjacent to a goat
2. The point directly beyond the goat (in the same direction) must be empty
3. The jump must follow a valid board connection line
4. Tiger lands on the empty point, goat is removed

**Example**:
```
T - G - .
```
Tiger (T) can jump over Goat (G) to empty point (.), capturing the goat.

**Restrictions**:
- Can only capture ONE goat per turn
- Cannot capture diagonally unless on a diagonal connection point
- Cannot capture if landing point is occupied or invalid

## Win Conditions

### Tigers Win If:
- **Capture 5 goats** (at any point in the game)
- This ends the game immediately

### Goats Win If:
- **Block all 4 tigers** so none of them can make a legal move
- This can only happen in the Movement Phase (after all goats are placed)
- Tigers must be completely surrounded/trapped

## Strategy

### Tiger Strategy 🐅
- **Control the center** - more mobility and capture opportunities
- **Maintain mobility** - don't get trapped in corners
- **Create threats** - force goats into vulnerable positions
- **Patience** - wait for good capture opportunities
- **Hunt strategically** - isolate and capture weak goats

### Goat Strategy 🐐
- **Build defensive formations** early
- **Work together** - use multiple goats to trap tigers
- **Control space** - limit tiger mobility
- **Strategic placement** - block key points
- **Avoid isolation** - keep goats connected
- **Sacrifice wisely** - sometimes losing a goat creates a winning trap

## Common Mistakes

### Tigers' Mistakes 🐅
- ❌ Rushing to capture without considering position
- ❌ Getting trapped in corners
- ❌ Losing mobility by moving to edge points unnecessarily
- ❌ Ignoring goat formations that will trap them

### Goats' Mistakes 🐐
- ❌ Placing goats randomly without a plan
- ❌ Leaving isolated goats
- ❌ Not using numbers advantage
- ❌ Failing to coordinate to trap tigers
- ❌ Giving up after losing a few goats

## Advanced Concepts

### Opening Theory
**Tigers**:
- Move toward center for maximum control
- Avoid corners except when necessary

**Goats**:
- Place first goats to control center
- Build walls to restrict tiger movement
- Create defensive structures early

### Middle Game
**Tigers**:
- Look for sacrifice patterns
- Create forcing sequences
- Hunt isolated goats

**Goats**:
- Complete encirclement patterns
- Coordinate multiple goats
- Maintain formation integrity

### Endgame
**Tigers**:
- Calculate forced captures
- Assess if 5 captures is still achievable
- Avoid stalemate patterns

**Goats**:
- Tighten the noose
- Complete the trap
- Verify no escape routes for tigers

## Game Notation (Optional)

Positions can be described using coordinates:
- **Rows**: 0-4 (top to bottom)
- **Columns**: 0-4 (left to right)

**Move notation**:
- Placement: `G → (2,2)` (goat placed at row 2, col 2)
- Movement: `T (0,0) → (0,1)` (tiger moves from 0,0 to 0,1)
- Capture: `T (1,1) → (3,3) x (2,2)` (tiger captures goat at 2,2)

## Cultural Significance

Bagh Chal is:
- 📜 **Historical** - Played in Nepal for centuries
- 🎓 **Educational** - Teaches strategic thinking
- 👨‍👩‍👧 **Social** - Played by all ages
- 🏛️ **Cultural heritage** - Part of Nepali tradition
- 🧠 **Mental exercise** - Improves planning and tactics

## Variants

### Traditional Rules
- Standard rules as described above

### Modern Variants
- **Time limits** - Each player has limited time
- **Different starting positions** - Tigers don't start in corners
- **More goats** - 24 or 25 goats instead of 20
- **Different win conditions** - Tigers need 6 captures

## Tips for Beginners

1. **Start with goats** - Easier to learn the game
2. **Focus on patterns** - Learn common formations
3. **Practice both sides** - Understand both perspectives
4. **Watch experienced players** - Learn strategies
5. **Analyze your games** - Understand mistakes
6. **Be patient** - Mastery takes time

## Why Goats Win More Often

In balanced play:
- Goats have a slight advantage
- 20 pieces vs 4 gives numerical superiority
- Proper defensive play is hard for tigers to break

**Balance tip**: Beginners often play tigers, experienced players play goats.
